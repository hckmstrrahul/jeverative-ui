What does Jev actually handle?

  • Page-level spatial arrangement, spacing density and surface choice.
  • Typed decisions from an allowed candidate set; local code enforces the scaffold.
  • It does not author the full component tree, write sample content, inspect screenshots or perform the correction stage.
  • Mint rules, accessibility fixes and the renderer are shared with LLM-only mode.

There is no defensible “percentage of intelligence” figure. In the supplied v4 benchmark, planning's median was 404ms versus 10,297ms total hybrid completion—roughly 4% as a ratio of medians, not per-run attribution.

Why your Claude bill grew

  • Your screenshot shows $4.19 across 505 API requests and 3.65M tokens; requests are not the same as completed screens.
  • A large rules/catalog prefix is repeated on text calls. The screenshot reports 0% cache hits.
  • Repairs add another text call. The previous retry also repeated up to 100K characters of output alongside the accepted document.
  • Now: budget default, optional reasoning disabled for presets, explicit Claude prefix caching and a 12K-character failed-output tail for repairs.

Does hybrid beat LLM-only?

It performed better on completion rate and successful-run median latency in your supplied run. That supports further testing; it does not prove universal superiority or better design.

At identical text-token counts, hybrid costs slightly more because it adds Jev. It becomes cheaper per usable screen only if it saves enough text work, retries or failed designs.

For Jev-like total cost, more content must come from deterministic templates or existing data. The legacy Jev-only mode does that, but gives up open-ended generation.
